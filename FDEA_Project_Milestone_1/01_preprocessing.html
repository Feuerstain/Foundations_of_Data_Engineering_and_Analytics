CT7 energy prediction: data pre-processing¶

Foundations of Data Engineering and Analytics, data analysis project · Lucas Alois Griesser, Luca Jenewein, Nikola Adžić · Winter Semester 2026/27

Research question. How accurately can the net battery energy of the CT7 over the next 5 seconds of driving be predicted from the driver inputs and vehicle state of the preceding 5 seconds, and do standard machine-learning models outperform a persistence baseline and a physics-based baseline on test days that were not used for training?

Target. The net battery energy of a 5-second window is E = Σ P · Δt over its 50 values (Δt = 0.1 s), in kJ, where P is the power at the battery output reported by the ISA current and voltage sensor (it agrees closely with U · I, see section 5). E > 0 means that the battery was discharged; E < 0 means that regenerative braking fed back more energy than the car used in that window.

Input. data/ct7_can_extract.csv.gz and data/ct7_sessions.csv, written by 00_extraction.ipynb from the raw CAN excerpt of the CT7 in data/raw/ (run that notebook first). They are decoded and reduced, not raw. Everything needed is part of the project package; this notebook does not need the original logs.

Output. data/ct7_clean_10hz.csv.gz (cleaned 10 Hz time series of all driving stints) and data/ct7_windows_5s.csv (one row per pair of consecutive 5-second windows; the dataset for EDA and modelling).

1. Data description and gathering¶

The CT7 is the 2025 electric race car of the Formula Student team CTM. A Raspberry Pi in the car records all frames of its four CAN buses (can-main, can-inv, can-sense, can-ams) with candump into one log file per power-up. Between 24 April and 16 August 2025 this produced 528 log files (about 31 GiB; the files before 7 May are empty). CTM allowed the use of the logs for this project and the submission of an excerpt of the raw frames together with the definitions of the selected messages.

The logs were scanned once (make_raw_excerpt.py): files named 26 May 2025 or later, at most one frame per message and 100 ms, and only periods in which a wheel motor turns faster than 100 1/min, plus 30 s before and after. The 130 earlier non-empty logs contain no driving with a battery power measurement: ISA frames appear in only 26 of them, and in the four of these in which a wheel motor turns, a single motor exceeds 100 1/min for at most 4 s while the battery current stays below 0.1 A. This check and the archive figures above come from the full logs, not from the package. The included excerpt holds the raw frames of the selected periods. 00_extraction.ipynb decodes them into 12 signals with the message definitions from CTM's DBC file. Timestamps are seconds since the earliest thinned frame of each log file; a session is one log file, and days are numbered D01, D02, ... in date order.

2. Timestamps, clock jumps and duplicates¶

Two clock effects were found by the scan of the full logs:

  1. The Raspberry Pi has no battery-backed clock. After booting, it continues from an outdated time and jumps forward once it gets the correct time over the network. Values before and after such a jump must never end up in the same window.
  2. Frames of different buses are not always written in time order (one bus is sometimes written later than the others; the scan counted 217 backward steps of more than 1 s in the sessions with data), so the file order is not the time order.

Checks: within one signal, the values must be in increasing time order in the file (otherwise the clock was set back), and no value may appear twice.

3. Invalid values: fixed bounds, sentinels, frozen values¶

Each signal has a range of physically possible values (fixed bounds). Values outside are set to missing, not clipped, because a corrupted value says nothing about the true value.

  • Cell temperature: exactly 0.000 °C is sent while the battery management system has no measurement (a sentinel value). The lowest real value is 26.3 °C.
  • Steering angle: −150.005° is the smallest 16-bit raw value (−32768) times the scale factor of the signal, a sentinel for "no valid angle". Values up to ±149.9° are real full-lock positions.
  • Motor speed and pedals: no value outside the bounds. Small negative motor speeds (the car rolling backwards) are kept.

Frozen values. A motor speed that stays exactly the same (with a magnitude above 300 1/min) for at least 5 s cannot be real, because the measured speed changes slightly every 100 ms. This happens when an inverter keeps sending its last value after a fault; such values are set to missing as well.

4. Time alignment on a 10 Hz grid¶

The signals arrive at different times and rates. For each recording block a regular 10 Hz grid is built, and every signal gets its most recent value that is at most 1 s old (merge_asof, backward). Older values count as missing, so a signal that stops sending does not keep its last value, and nothing is carried from one block into another. Values set to missing in section 3 are left out of the merge, so a row takes the last valid value if it is at most 1 s old.

5. Consistency of power, voltage and current; sign convention¶

The ISA sensor reports the power P in addition to the voltage U and the current I, so P should be close to U · I. The three values come in separate CAN messages at different rates, so on the grid they can be up to 0.1 s apart. If the remaining differences come from this timing and not from wrong values, they must be smallest when the series are compared without a time shift, and they must average out over a window.

7. Segmentation into driving stints¶

A stint is a continuous drive inside one recording block: the mean motor speed (of at least three motors) is above 300 1/min, stops shorter than 20 s do not end it, and it lasts at least 10 s. Rows outside stints (standing, the margins kept around activity) are dropped. In addition, a stint is only used if the battery delivered more than 1 kW at least once; otherwise the wheels turned without drive power (probably because the car was pushed or rolled with the high-voltage system switched off).

In [14]:
def stint_ids(block):
    """Number the stints of one block; rows outside a stint get NaN."""
    t, move = block.t_s.to_numpy(), (block.rpm_mean > MOVING_RPM).to_numpy()
    ids, n, start, end = np.full(len(block), np.nan), 0, None, None
    for i in np.flatnonzero(move):
        if end is not None and t[i] - t[end] > MAX_PAUSE_S:  # stop too long: close the stint
            if t[end] - t[start] >= MIN_STINT_S:
                ids[start:end + 1] = n
                n += 1
            start = None
        if start is None:
            start = i
        end = i
    if start is not None and t[end] - t[start] >= MIN_STINT_S:
        ids[start:end + 1] = n
    return pd.Series(ids, index=block.index)

8. Feature windows and target¶

Each stint is cut into consecutive, non-overlapping windows of 5 s (50 grid rows) from its start; an incomplete last window is dropped. Each row of the final dataset pairs a window w (features) with the following window w+1 of the same stint (target). Features use only values of window w:

Feature Definition over window w
rpm_mean mean of the mean motor speed
rpm_delta mean motor speed at the end minus at the start of the window (acceleration proxy; missing if one of the two is missing)
accel_mean, accel_max mean and maximum accelerator pedal position
brake_mean, regen_mean mean brake pedal position and mean regeneration request
steer_abs_mean mean absolute steering angle (missing until 21 June 2025)
voltage_start battery voltage at the start of the window (state of charge proxy)
cell_temp_max highest cell temperature in the window
net_energy_kj net battery energy of window w
t_in_stint_s time since the start of the stint

Target: net_energy_next_kj, the net battery energy of window w+1 in kJ. Battery current, voltage and power of window w+1 are not used as features, and motor torques or currents were not extracted at all, because they determine the power almost directly. A window is used only if its power values are complete, so that its energy is not computed over a gap.

Pairs in which a window differs by more than 2 kJ and 20 % are removed, because one of the values is not reliable there.     """Energy from P differs from the energy from U*I by more than 2 kJ and 20 %."""

    return (diff > 2) & (diff / energy_ui.abs().clip(lower=1) > 0.2)

9. Bias and sampling¶

The dataset is not a random sample of how the car is driven:

  • Unequal days. The number of windows differs strongly between days (table below), and neighbouring windows of the same stint are not independent (speed, battery voltage and temperature change little from one window to the next). A model evaluated on randomly mixed windows would mainly learn the large days and look better than it is (random cross-validation underestimates the error for dependent data, Roberts et al. 2017). Evaluation will therefore always hold out whole days (leave-one-day-out), and results will also be reported per day.
  • Test drives only. All data come from test drives, not from competition events; how much of the driving is endurance-like is unknown.
  • Selection by the activity filter. The extraction kept only periods in which a wheel motor turned faster than 100 1/min, plus 30 s before and after. Every stint row lies within 21 s of such a motor speed (moving rows within 1 s, stops shorter than 20 s), so the filter removes no driving, only standing and very slow rolling. While the car stands within the kept periods (8.5 h), the battery power is close to zero (second table below), so the standing time that was not extracted very likely holds little energy as well; this cannot be measured from the extract.
  • Selection by the stint rules. Slow manoeuvring below 300 1/min, stops longer than 20 s and rolling without drive power are excluded, so the dataset describes powered driving only. 98.5 % of the net battery energy in the kept periods lies in stints; most of the rest (2.0 MJ) falls in rows with fewer than three motor speeds. A wheel motor turned on 20 days, but only 11 of them contain usable powered driving (first table below): on D01 only the front motors were logged, and on the other eight days the car drove above 300 1/min for less than a minute or without drive power.
  • Sampling. Windows do not overlap, so each value of the 10 Hz series is used in at most one feature window and one target window. No further subsampling is applied.
